# OpenMimic Core Concepts

This document explains the key abstractions in OpenMimic and points to the code that implements each one.

---

## Testimony Ledger

The testimony ledger is the append-only store of all witness statements. Each entry records the witness's relationship to the subject, their verbatim answers, and their consent level. SQLite triggers physically block UPDATE and DELETE operations on the testimony table. The TypeScript API (`kernel/src/store.ts`) exposes no methods to modify existing entries. AI-generated content (room transcripts, court outputs) is stored in separate tables and never written back into the ledger.

**Code:** `kernel/src/store.ts` (schema, triggers, putTestimony), `shared/src/schemas.ts` (TestimonySchema).

---

## Episode

An episode is a specific anecdote or event extracted from testimony. It must be a **verbatim sub-string** of the original testimony text -- the court filing stage enforces this. Episodes are the most concrete form of evidence: they record what happened, in whose words, in what context. Persona assembly allocates 50% of the prompt budget to episodes because ablation testing shows they contribute more to prediction quality than abstracted claims.

**Code:** `shared/src/schemas.ts` (EpisodeSchema), `engines/court/src/court.ts` (filing stage extraction).

---

## Situated Claim

A claim is an assertion about the subject derived from testimony -- e.g. "She avoids conflict by going silent." Each claim carries:

- `evidence`: a non-empty list of testimony IDs that support it (enforced; `putClaim` throws `NoEvidenceError` if empty or if IDs do not exist in the ledger).
- `witnessIds`: which witnesses contributed.
- `episodeIds`: which episodes anchor it.
- `conviction`: a confidence score computed by a pure function (base 0.5, adjusted by witness count, episode presence, pairing status).
- `status`: one of `surviving`, `contested`, `retired`, `qualified`.
- `kind`: `fact`, `observation`, or `pattern`.
- `context`: who the claim is about in relation to whom, in what setting.

Claims are situated: they describe the subject *as seen by specific people in specific circumstances*, not as a universal truth.

**Code:** `shared/src/schemas.ts` (ClaimSchema, ClaimStatusSchema), `engines/court/src/court.ts` (computeConviction), `kernel/src/store.ts` (putClaim).

---

## Divergence

When the court finds that two witnesses describe the same behavioral dimension differently -- not a factual contradiction, but a difference in perspective -- it creates a divergence record preserving both viewpoints rather than choosing one. For example, a mother says "he's steady" and a boss says "he's impulsive" about the same person's decision-making. The divergence record stores both claims, both witnesses, the behavioral dimension, and the resolution type (`kept_both` for perspective differences, `contested` for unresolved factual conflicts).

The engine does not decide who is right. This is a design principle, not a limitation: personality psychology (Mischel & Shoda 1995, CAPS model) treats stable if-then behavioral signatures as the unit of personality. Different witnesses seeing different facets is the expected case, not noise to be eliminated.

**Code:** `shared/src/schemas.ts` (DivergenceSchema), `engines/court/src/court.ts` (relation judgment stage), `web/src/views/CourtReportView.vue` (display: blue for perspective, red for factual conflict).

---

## Expression Tiers

Each utterance in a room is classified into one of three tiers that describe how closely it tracks the source testimony:

- **quote**: verbatim or near-verbatim from testimony by a `quotable` witness.
- **paraphrase**: restates testimony content in different words; anchored to specific testimony entries.
- **extrapolate**: plausible speech not directly anchored to any specific testimony.

The tier is determined by `deriveExpressionTier` before generation (setting a cap) and verified by `classifyUtterance` after generation. `synthesis_only` witnesses are capped at paraphrase. Contested, retired, or subject-denied claims are capped at extrapolate.

**Code:** `engines/room/src/tier.ts` (deriveExpressionTier, classifyUtterance), `engines/room/src/room.ts` (runSchedule enforcement).

---

## No-Talk List

Before a behind-the-scenes room session, the RoomEngine generates a no-talk list: topics that witnesses must not disclose in the room. The list targets only high-cost secrets -- information that was explicitly entrusted in confidence *and* that would overturn another present witness's understanding of a significant situation. The list is generated by two LLM passes (union, capped at 8 items). Rule-based entries (e.g. explicitly marked confidential testimony) are included regardless of the cap.

Leak detection runs in two stages: keyword fast-scan followed by LLM semantic judgment (including partial leak detection). If a leak is detected, the engine attempts a guided rewrite using safe material (up to 2 retries), falling back to a stage direction if rewrite fails. A room session enforces a 25% stage-direction cap.

**Limitation:** The no-talk list is regenerated each session and may vary -- it has produced as few as 1 item for a fixture with multiple secrets. Euphemistic wording can bypass keyword detection. The face-to-face room's integration with the no-talk list and leak verification is still being completed. Whether something counts as a "partial leak" depends on the model's judgment.

**Code:** `engines/room/src/room.ts` (no-talk generation, leak detection, guided rewrite), `engines/room/src/wordlist.ts` (crisis/diagnosis word lists).

## Meta-Perception

The meta-perception plugin lets the subject predict how each witness answered specific questions, then scores the predictions against the actual testimony using an LLM judge. Results are broken down per-witness. For `synthesis_only` witnesses, identifying details are masked in the output.

This maps to the psychological concept of meta-accuracy -- how well a person knows how others see them (Kenny & DePaulo 1993; Carlson, Vazire & Furr 2011).

**Code:** `plugins/meta-perception/` (prediction, scoring, per-witness breakdown).

---

## Reflux Detection

AI reflux is when AI-generated output (room narratives, court claims) gets recycled back into the system as if it were original testimony. The reflux detector uses 3-character shingle MinHash fingerprinting (128 dimensions) to detect verbatim and near-verbatim matches. AI artifacts are fingerprinted at generation time; incoming testimony is screened against registered fingerprints. Medium and high suspicion testimony is excluded from court filing.

**Limitation:** Paraphrased reflux below the MinHash Jaccard threshold (0.5) will not be caught.

**Code:** `kernel/src/reflux.ts` (fingerprinting, screening), `engines/court/src/court.ts` (filing-stage filtering), `engines/room/src/room.ts` (fingerprint registration).

---

## Consent Levels

Each witness chooses a consent level when submitting testimony:

- **quotable**: verbatim text may appear in rooms, exports, biography output, and persona packages.
- **synthesis_only**: raw text is replaced with `[withheld]` in any external-facing scope. The testimony still contributes to claims and court processing, but the original words are not shown.

The consent gate (`kernel/src/gate.ts`) enforces this distinction at the API layer. Persona packages exclude `synthesis_only` raw text. The biography plugin checks for synthesis_only leakage (8+ consecutive matching characters).

**Code:** `shared/src/schemas.ts` (ConsentLevelSchema), `kernel/src/gate.ts` (synthesis_only masking), `server/src/persona-package.ts` (export filtering).

---

## Disclosure Levels

Disclosure levels control how a claim appears in the persona prompt. They are a separate axis from consent:

- **speakable**: the persona may use this claim freely.
- **reference_only**: annotated in the prompt but not to be raised proactively.
- **presence_only**: included only as context, not to be spoken about directly.
- **excluded**: filtered out of the persona entirely.

`holdUntilRaised` is orthogonal: the persona must not raise the topic proactively, but may respond if asked. `deriveDisclosure` is a pure function.

**Code:** `kernel/src/disclosure.ts` (deriveDisclosure), `kernel/src/persona.ts` (per-claim filtering during assembly).

---

## Corpus

The corpus stores the subject's own words -- not testimony about them, but things they actually said or wrote (e.g. chat messages, social media posts). Corpus items are stored in a separate table from testimony and serve two purposes: providing a speaking-style reference for persona generation, and populating the "what they said in their own words" section of the persona prompt and biography output.

**Code:** `shared/src/schemas.ts` (CorpusItemSchema), `kernel/src/store.ts` (putCorpusItem, listCorpusItemsBySubject), `kernel/src/persona.ts` (corpus section in assembly).
